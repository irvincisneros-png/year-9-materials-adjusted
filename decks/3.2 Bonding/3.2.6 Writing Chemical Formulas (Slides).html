BONDING · FORMULAS

Chemical Formula

Uses element symbols and small subscript numbers
Shows which atoms are present and how many
E.g. H2O = 2 hydrogen atoms + 1 oxygen atom
BONDING · FORMULAS

Ionic vs Covalent

Ionic: formula shows the simplest ratio of ions with total charge zero
Covalent: formula shows the atoms in one molecule
Different rules: ionic ratios vs atoms in a molecule
IONIC FORMULAS · CROSS-OVER RULE

Cross-Over Shortcut for Ionic Formulas

Step 1: Write the positive ion first, then the negative ion and their charges
Step 2: Use charge magnitudes as temporary subscripts
Step 3: Remove charge signs; simplify to the lowest whole-number ratio
CROSS-OVER RULE · WORKED EXAMPLE

Ionic Formula: Ca2+ and Cl−

Ca has charge 2+; Cl has charge 1−
Use charge magnitudes: 1 Ca ion for every 2 Cl ions
Formula = CaCl2. Check: +2 + 2×(−1) = 0 ✓

COVALENT FORMULAS · VALENCY

Covalent Formulas

Valency = the usual number of bonds an atom forms
Water: O forms 2 bonds; each H forms 1 → H2O
Methane: C forms 4 bonds; each H forms 1 → CH4
FORMULAS · CHECKING YOUR WORK

Always Check Your Formula

Ionic: total positive charge = total negative charge
Covalent: each atom has its usual number of bonds
Write ionic formulas in the lowest whole-number ratio (Ca2Cl2 → CaCl2)
TEACHER-LED PRACTICE

Write the ionic formula

Write the cation first, balance charges, then simplify the ratio.

Mg2+ + O2− → ____
Al3+ + O2− → ____
Na+ + S2− → ____
Ca2+ + Cl− → ____

Check: total positive charge = total negative charge.
