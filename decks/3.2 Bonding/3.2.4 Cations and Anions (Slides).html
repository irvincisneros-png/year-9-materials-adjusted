What Is an Ion?

An ion is a charged atom or group of atoms
Ions form when atoms or groups gain or lose electrons
Electron transfer between atoms is one common route
BONDING

Cations — Positive Ions

Atom LOSES electrons → cation forms
More protons than electrons → positive charge
Metals usually form cations (e.g. Na⁺, Mg²⁺)
BONDING

Anions — Negative Ions

Atom GAINS electrons → anion forms
More electrons than protons → negative charge
Non-metals usually form anions (e.g. Cl⁻, O²⁻)

Cation vs Anion

Cation (+)
Loses electrons
More protons than electrons
Formed by metals
Anion (−)
Gains electrons
More electrons than protons
Formed by non-metals
BONDING

Sodium and Chlorine: An Example

Na: 11 p⁺, 11 e⁻ → loses 1 e⁻ → Na⁺ (10 e⁻)
Cl: 17 p⁺, 17 e⁻ → gains 1 e⁻ → Cl⁻ (18 e⁻)
Charge check: +1 and −1 balance in a 1:1 ratio
BONDING

Magnesium and Oxygen

Mg: 12 p⁺, 12 e⁻ → loses 2 e⁻ → Mg²⁺ (10 e⁻)
O: 8 p⁺, 8 e⁻ → gains 2 e⁻ → O²⁻ (10 e⁻)
For main-group elements, common ions are a useful shortcut; always check charge
