🌍 REAL WORLD LINK

Oxygen you breathe is O₂, not O

O₂ and N₂ are major gases in air

Every breath you take contains O₂ and N₂ — diatomic molecules. Writing the wrong formula in a chemical equation can change the whole reaction.

BONDING

What Does Diatomic Mean?

Di = two, atomic = atoms — so two atoms bonded together
Elements can occur as atoms, molecules or extended structures
H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂ and I₂ are diatomic at standard conditions
BONDING

The seven diatomic elements

H₂  N₂  F₂  O₂  I₂  Cl₂  Br₂
All are non-metals found in Group 17 or are gases
Two atoms of the same element share electrons
MEMORY TIP

The Mnemonic — Never Forget!

Have No Fear Of Icy Cold Breezes
H₂   N₂   F₂   O₂   I₂   Cl₂  Br₂
First letter of each word = the element symbol
BONDING

Why Do They Bond in Pairs?

In elemental forms under standard conditions, these elements commonly form covalent X₂ molecules
Two atoms share electrons: this is a covalent bond
Sharing gives each atom a full outer shell — stable!
BONDING

Oxygen: A Double Bond

One O atom has 6 outer electrons — needs 2 more
Two O atoms share 2 pairs of electrons (double bond)
Each O ends up with 8 outer electrons — stable
PROPERTIES

Physical States at Room Temp

H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂ — all gases at room temperature
Br₂ is a liquid at room temperature
I₂ is a solid — but still exists as I₂ molecules
WRITING EQUATIONS

Writing equations

For elemental oxygen under standard conditions, write O₂ in equations
For elemental hydrogen under standard conditions, write H₂ in equations
A wrong formula can give an incorrect equation and atom balance
