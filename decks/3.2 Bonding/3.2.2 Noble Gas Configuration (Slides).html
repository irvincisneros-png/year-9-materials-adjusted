Why Atoms Bond

Bonding can make an arrangement more stable.

Electron transfer can form ions. In salt, oppositely charged ions attract in a repeating ionic lattice.

NOBLE GASES

Meet the noble gases

Noble gases are in Group 18 of the periodic table.
Examples include helium, neon, argon, krypton, xenon and radon.
They are generally very unreactive because their outer shells are full.
ELECTRON SHELLS

Stable noble gases

Their outer electron shells are full.
Helium has 2 electrons in its filled first shell.
The other noble gases have 8 outer-shell electrons in this model.
KEY CONCEPT

Noble-gas configuration

A filled outer shell is a useful stability model for many main-group atoms.
Atoms can lose, gain or share electrons in simple bonding models.
Noble-gas configuration is a shortcut—not the sole reason all bonds form.

This models ions, not a single NaCl molecule.
BONDING TYPES

Ionic vs covalent

Ionic models show electron transfer, then attraction between oppositely charged ions.
Covalent models show atoms sharing electron pairs.
Both often give main-group atoms filled outer shells in this model; H and He use 2.
OCTET RULE

The octet pattern

The octet is a useful main-group pattern, not a rule for every atom.
Many main-group atoms form bonds that give them 8 outer electrons in this model.
Hydrogen and helium have a filled first shell with 2 electrons.
