🌍 REAL WORLD LINK

Why Bonds Matter

Bonds explain why materials behave differently

Bonding and structure strongly influence how materials behave: salt may dissolve, metals can bend, and plastics soften at different temperatures.

BONDING BASICS

What Is a Chemical Bond?

A bond is an attraction that holds atoms together
Ionic bonding is attraction between ions after electron transfer
Covalent and metallic bonding involve shared or delocalised electrons
IONIC BONDING

Ionic Bonds

Metal + non-metal atoms involved
Metal loses electrons → positive ion (cation)
Non-metal gains electrons → negative ion (anion)
IONIC EXAMPLE

Ionic Bond Example: Salt (NaCl)

Sodium (Na) gives 1 electron to chlorine (Cl)
Na becomes Na+ ; Cl becomes Cl−
Opposite charges attract — that is the ionic bond
COVALENT BONDING

Covalent Bonds

Two non-metal atoms share electrons
Both atoms are attracted to the shared pair
Example: water (H2O) and carbon dioxide (CO2)

Ionic vs Covalent

Ionic
Metal + non-metal
Electrons transferred
High melting point
Covalent
Non-metal + non-metal
Electrons shared
Usually low melting point
METALLIC BONDING

Metallic Bonds

Found in pure metals (Cu, Fe, Al)
Positive metal ions in a ‘sea’ of free electrons
Free electrons allow electricity to flow

METALLIC PROPERTIES

Why Metals Are Special

Sea of electrons → good conductor of electricity
Layers of ions can slide → metals are malleable
Strong attraction → metals have high melting points
