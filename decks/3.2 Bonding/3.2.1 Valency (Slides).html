How atoms bond to become stable

🌍 REAL WORLD LINK

Why Valency Matters

Atoms form bonds when the arrangement is more stable.

Valency helps predict common combining patterns.

BONDING

What is valency?

Valency is an atom’s typical combining capacity.
It often matches the number of bonds formed in simple compounds.
Valency is not the same thing as ionic charge.
BONDING

The octet pattern

The octet is a useful main-group pattern, not a rule for every atom.
Hydrogen and helium use a full first shell of 2 electrons.
Noble gases already have full outer shells and are often assigned valency 0.
PERIODIC TABLE

Group patterns

Main-group position is a useful shortcut for outer-electron patterns.
Group 1 commonly forms +1 ions; Group 2 commonly forms +2 ions.
Groups 16 and 17 often form −2 and −1 ions in simple ionic compounds.

Loss, gain and sharing

Metals (ions)
Na commonly loses 1 electron → Na⁺
Mg commonly loses 2 electrons → Mg²⁺
Ionic charge records electron loss or gain.
Non-metals (ions / bonds)
Cl commonly gains 1 electron → Cl⁻
O commonly gains 2 electrons → O²⁻
Carbon usually forms covalent bonds by sharing.
BONDING

Common valency patterns

H = 1, Na = 1, Mg = 2, C = 4
O = 2, Cl = 1, Ne = 0 (noble gas)
Elements in a group often show similar patterns; transition metals can vary.

BONDING

Carbon is special

Carbon has 4 valence electrons.
It commonly forms four covalent bonds by sharing electrons.
This lets carbon form chains, rings and many molecular structures.
