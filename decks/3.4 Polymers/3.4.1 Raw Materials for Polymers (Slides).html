Plastic is Everywhere

But where does it come from?

Plastic bags, bottles and food wrap are all polymers. Knowing their raw materials helps us find greener alternatives.

BUILDING BLOCKS

Monomers and Polymers

Mono = one; poly = many
A monomer is one small unit
A polymer is a long chain of monomers
REACTION

Polymerisation

Polymerisation joins monomers into a chain
Thousands of monomers link up
The result is a long polymer chain; properties depend on its structure
▶ TRY IT - POLYMERISE

Add Monomers to Build a Polymer

n = 1

Many ethene monomers join to make one long polyethene chain. Its properties depend on structure and arrangement.

Monomer vs Polymer

Monomer
One small unit
Small and simple
e.g. ethene (a gas)
Polymer
Long chain of many units
Larger macromolecule; properties depend on structure
e.g. polyethylene (a plastic)
RAW MATERIALS

Common Fossil Feedstocks for Plastics

Many widely used plastics use monomers from crude oil or natural gas
Ethene can be produced from fossil feedstocks, then polymerised to make polyethylene (PE)
PE is used in products such as film, bottles and pipes; grade and design vary
BIOPLASTICS

Bio-based and Biodegradable Are Different

Some polymers use bio-based feedstocks rather than fossil feedstocks
PLA is commonly made from lactic acid produced by fermenting plant sugars, including corn-derived sugars
Bio-based and biodegradable are distinct; benefits depend on feedstock, production and disposal conditions

Polymer Raw Materials Summary

Polymer Monomer Feedstock route
Polyethylene (PE) Ethene Crude oil / natural gas
Polypropylene (PP) Propene Crude oil / natural gas
PLA (commonly bio-based) Lactic acid Plant-derived sugars (sometimes corn-derived) → fermentation → lactic acid → PLA
