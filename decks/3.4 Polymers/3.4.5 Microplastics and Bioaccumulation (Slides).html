Plastic is Inside Us

Microplastics have been found in human blood

Scientists have found microplastics in human blood, lungs, and organs — from seafood, drinking water, and even the air we breathe.

MICROPLASTICS

What Are Microplastics?

Plastic pieces smaller than 5 mm
Form when large plastic breaks down over years
Found in oceans, rivers, soil, and living things

Two Ways Microplastics Form

Made Small on Purpose
Historically, microbeads were used in some rinse-off cosmetics
Rules differ by region; many places have restricted these uses
Added to cosmetics
Break Down Over Time
Sunlight breaks plastic apart
Wind and waves shred plastic
Plastic bags and bottles
KEY IDEA

Bioaccumulation

A harmful substance builds up in a body over time
The body cannot remove it fast enough
Particles or associated chemicals may be retained; evidence varies by organism, particle and dose
KEY IDEA

Biomagnification

When prey is eaten, the predator gets ALL its plastic
Biomagnification means concentration increases across trophic levels when measurements show it
Do not assume this occurs for every microplastic, food web or setting
▶ TRY IT - BIOACCUM

Step Up the Food Chain

Plastics are not easily excreted, so concentration increases up the chain.

CASE STUDIES

Case Studies — Who Is Affected?

Case studies: effects must be linked to a measured exposure, dose and species
Reef fish: less feeding and slower growth
Evidence model: map source → exposure → ingestion → measured outcome
HUMANS

Human Exposure: What We Know

Microplastics found in human blood and organs
Sources: seafood, drinking water, air
Detection or exposure does not by itself demonstrate harm or biomagnification
PRACTICE

Microplastics Food-Web Practice

Trace the evidence before making a food-web claim.

ExposureParticles can enter an environment from a source.
IngestionSome organisms can take particles in while feeding.
BioaccumulationLook for evidence of a substance building up in one organism over time.
BiomagnificationLook for measured increases across trophic levels; do not assume this is universal.
