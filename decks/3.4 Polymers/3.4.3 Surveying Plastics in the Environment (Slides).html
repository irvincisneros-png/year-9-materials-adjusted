Plastic Survey

Surveying litter in the environment

🎯 LEARNING INTENTION

We are learning to survey and identify types of plastic litter in our local area

🌍 REAL WORLD LINK

Environmental concern needs evidence

Match the size of the claim to the evidence

Global studies identify plastic leakage as a widespread issue, but figures vary with the year, method and boundary. Our class survey records only what was visible in one sampled place and time.

PERSISTENCE

Fragmenting is not disappearing

Many common plastics are not readily biodegradable in ordinary environments
Weathering can fragment plastic into smaller pieces; that is not the same as chemical disappearance
Persistence varies with the material and conditions—avoid one lifetime for every item
METHOD

A Physical Survey Gives Local Evidence

Walk a defined area and count visible plastic items by category
The tally is an observation about this sampled place and time
It cannot by itself prove where litter came from or describe the whole suburb
TECHNIQUE

Being Systematic

Systematic means working in an orderly, set pattern
Walk in a zigzag so you cover every part of the area
Avoid missing spots or counting the same item twice
RECORDING DATA

Tally First; Interpret Second

Record each visible item in a defined category (bottle, bag, wrapper)
Use tally marks: four lines then a diagonal for five
State the sample when comparing counts; a tally alone does not show the source of litter

Common Types of Plastic Litter

Hard Plastics
PET drink bottles
Bottle caps
Polystyrene foam pieces
Soft / Flexible Plastics
LDPE plastic bags
Food wrappers / packets
Other flexible film
BEFORE YOU GO OUT

Survey Safety Rules

Always wear disposable gloves
Do NOT touch sharp objects or broken glass
Wash hands with soap and water afterwards
TEACHER-LED PRACTICE

Sort the claim by its evidence

Label each statement: direct observation, supported comparison, or inference needing more evidence.

1 · Observation
“We counted 14 bottle caps in Area A.”
2 · Comparison
“Caps were more common than bags in Area A on survey day.”
3 · Inference
“Bottle caps came from the nearby sports field.”
4 · Overreach
“Caps are the biggest pollution problem in the whole suburb.”

Discuss: What extra evidence would make claims 3 and 4 stronger?

Three Things to Remember

Persistence

Many plastics can persist and fragment; rate depends on material and conditions

Systematic Survey

A planned route improves coverage, but every survey is still a sample

Evidence

State what your tally shows about the sampled area; label inference as inference
