HYDROCARBONS

What Are Alkanes?

Alkanes are hydrocarbons — only C and H atoms
All bonds between carbons are single bonds
Each carbon atom always makes exactly 4 bonds
GENERAL FORMULA

The General Formula: CnH(2n+2)

n = the number of carbon atoms in the chain
Hydrogens = 2 × carbons + 2
E.g. methane n=1: H = 2×1+2 = 4 → CH4

STRUCTURAL FORMULA

Structural Formula of Methane

Methane: 1 carbon bonded to 4 hydrogens
Carbon sits in the centre — H on all 4 sides
Every line between atoms = one covalent bond

Short vs Long Alkane Chains

C1–C4
Gases at room temperature
Lower boiling points
Used as cooking / heating fuel
C5–C8
Liquids at room temperature
Higher boiling points
Used as petrol and lubricants
INTERMOLECULAR FORCES

Why Boiling Point Increases

Longer chains = bigger molecules
Bigger molecules have stronger forces between them
More energy needed to separate them → higher BP
