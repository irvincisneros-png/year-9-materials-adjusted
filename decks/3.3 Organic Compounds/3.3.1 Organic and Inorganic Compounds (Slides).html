Organic Chemistry Is Everywhere

From fuels to food to your own body

Organic compounds make up living things, the food you eat, and the fuels that power cars. Knowing the difference helps chemists make medicines and plastics.

ORGANIC COMPOUNDS

What Is an Organic Compound?

Most organic compounds are carbon compounds; many contain C–H bonds
Found in living things and made by industry
Examples: methane (CH4), glucose (C6H12O6)
INORGANIC COMPOUNDS

What Is an Inorganic Compound?

Includes salts, metals, minerals and many carbon compounds
Common examples: NaCl, H2O, Fe2O3
Other inorganic carbon compounds include CO2, CO and carbonates
THE CLASSIFICATION RULE

A useful clue

C–H bond present? → strong clue: often organic
No C–H bond? → check the compound type
CO, CO2 and carbonates are common inorganic examples.
TRICKY CASE

The Tricky Case: CO2 and CO

CO2 and CO are carbon oxides — common inorganic carbon compounds
They are inorganic because they are carbon oxides
Carbon alone ≠ organic
