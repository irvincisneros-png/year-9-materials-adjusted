🌍 REAL WORLD LINK

Fuels Power Our World

But burning them has risks

Gas stoves, car engines and heaters all burn hydrocarbons. How they burn determines what waste products are made — some are harmful or even deadly.

ORGANIC CHEMISTRY

What is Combustion?

Combustion = fuel reacts with oxygen
Releases heat and light energy
Hydrocarbons contain carbon and hydrogen
COMPLETE COMBUSTION

Complete Combustion

Plenty of oxygen available
Products: carbon dioxide + water
Maximum energy released; blue flame
INCOMPLETE COMBUSTION

Incomplete Combustion

Limited oxygen supply
May produce carbon monoxide and/or soot + water (often some carbon dioxide)
Less energy released; yellow/orange flame

Complete vs Incomplete

Complete
Plenty of oxygen
CO2 + H2O produced
Blue flame; more energy
Incomplete
Limited oxygen
May produce CO and/or soot + H2O (often some CO2)
Yellow flame; less energy

SAFETY

Carbon monoxide (CO)

Colourless and odourless — you can't detect it
Stops blood carrying oxygen around the body
Always ventilate rooms with gas appliances

Bunsen Burner as a Model
