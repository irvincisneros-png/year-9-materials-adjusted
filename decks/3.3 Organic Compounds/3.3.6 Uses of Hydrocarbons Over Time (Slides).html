Why Does This Matter?

Hydrocarbons shape modern life

Some packaging, synthetic fibres and phone components use hydrocarbons. Many everyday materials do not.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT

1800s: Lighting and Coal

Whale oil was used for lighting in some regions; commercial whaling was harmful
Coal powered steam engines, railways and heating
Energy use varied by place: wood and water also mattered
ENERGY TRANSITION

1900s: Petroleum in U.S. Transport

U.S. oil booms increased petroleum supply
Petroleum became a major transport fuel
In the U.S., petroleum surpassed coal in 1950 — not everywhere

Coal and Petroleum: Changing Uses

COAL
Mined solid fuel
Steam, heating and electricity
Still important in some regions
PETROLEUM
Liquid fuel, pumped from underground
Powered cars and aeroplanes
Used for petrol and diesel
NEW APPLICATIONS

Hydrocarbons as Feedstocks

Feedstock = raw material for new chemicals
Petroleum and gas liquids can make plastics and solvents
Natural gas helps make many fertilisers; not all are hydrocarbon-derived

Hydrocarbon Use Through History

CLIMATE & ENERGY

21st Century: Reducing Combustion Fuel Use

Burning hydrocarbons releases CO2; fossil-fuel operations can also emit methane
Clean electricity, efficiency and electric transport can reduce combustion-fuel demand in some uses
Some petrochemical feedstock uses remain difficult to replace and are actively changing
GUIDED PRACTICE

Evaluate a Historical Claim

With a partner, decide: supported, or needs a qualifier?

CLAIM

“Coal was replaced everywhere by petroleum.”

Needs a qualifier: U.S. petroleum surpassed coal in 1950; patterns differed by region and sector.

CLAIM

“Petroleum is only a transport fuel.”

Not supported: it is also a feedstock for chemicals and materials.
