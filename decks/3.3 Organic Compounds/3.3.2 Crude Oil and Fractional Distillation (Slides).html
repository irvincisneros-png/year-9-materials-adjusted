Everything Runs on Crude Oil

From fuel to roads to plastics

Petrol in cars, jet fuel in planes, bitumen on roads — it all starts as crude oil. Fractional distillation is the first step that makes this possible.

ORGANIC CHEMISTRY

What Is Crude Oil?

A mixture of many different hydrocarbons
Hydrocarbons contain ONLY carbon and hydrogen
Formed from tiny sea organisms over millions of years
ORGANIC CHEMISTRY

What Is a Hydrocarbon?

A molecule made of carbon (C) and hydrogen (H) only
Short carbon chains = lower boiling point, more volatile
Long carbon chains = higher boiling point, more viscous
ORGANIC CHEMISTRY

How Fractional Distillation Works

Crude oil is heated and vaporised at the bottom of a tall tower
The column is hottest at the bottom and coolest at the top
Fractions condense at different heights by boiling range

Short Chains vs Long Chains

Short
Low boiling point
Lighter liquids or gases
Example: petrol, refinery gas
Long
High boiling point
Thick, viscous substances
Example: bitumen, lube oil

The Six Main Fractions

Fraction Position in column Common use
Refinery gas Top (lowest boiling point) Bottled gas for heating and cooking
Petrol Upper Fuel for cars
Kerosene Middle Jet fuel
Diesel Middle-lower Fuel for trucks and buses
Lubricating oil Lower Lubricants, oils and waxes
Bitumen Bottom (highest boiling point) Surfacing roads
